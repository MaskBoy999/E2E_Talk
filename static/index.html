With a value above 0, that many minutes without mouse/keyboard/touch activity wipes ALL local data (keys, logins, settings) and signs you out. Notification Privacy

Always on for every notification, on every device.

Every notification says only that something happened — no sender name, no channel or server, and no message text. Your operating system stores whatever a notification says (Windows keeps it in its notification database; a phone keeps it in the notification shade, and can show it on the lock screen), so a decrypted preview handed to the OS outlives the app. Keeping decrypted content out of the OS is not optional here: notifications still arrive, still buzz with your chosen sound, and still open the right conversation.

The incoming-call card obeys the same rule: it says a call is coming in, never who is calling, and the phone's own lock screen gets no name either.

Key Vault

Your storage key — the one that decrypts everything this app keeps on this device — is sealed with your password (Argon2id). This setting decides whether the app asks for that password again after a restart.

Checking…

Changing this asks for your password first, and it is checked against the vault: everything this app has stored on this device is then encrypted with the key that password releases, so the setting cannot be flipped without it.

On (recommended): a new tab or a restarted app shows Unlock your key vault and nothing is readable from this device's storage until your password is typed. Off: the app keeps that same key on this device, so a restart opens straight into the app — convenient, but anyone who can read this browser profile or app storage (a local attacker, another app on a rooted phone, a device backup) can decrypt your stored session without your password. Your messages are still end-to-end encrypted in transit and the server still sees only ciphertext, and the vault blob is kept either way, so you can turn this back on at any time.

Noise Suppression

RNNoise + Gate chains the neural model with a noise gate and compressor for the deepest on-device suppression.

Voice Activation

Runs the energy gate that is already part of the RNNoise chain: below the speech threshold your mic sends silence instead of your room. Keeps keyboards, TVs and other people off the call and uses less bandwidth. Needs an RNNoise mode — it does nothing with "Browser built-in" or "Off".

Your mic stays closed until you hold a call's mic button, in any call view. Nothing is transmitted while the button is up, and the button lights up for as long as you hold it.

A global key, so it talks to the same gate while the app sits in the background. Leave it blank for Ctrl+Shift+Space. It only registers while "Hold to talk" is on.

Audio Output

Where call audio comes out: speakers, headphones, a wired headset or Bluetooth. On Android this uses the phone's own call-audio routing (earpiece / speaker / Bluetooth); on desktop it picks the output device per audio element. Applied live — remote audio re-routes without rejoining.

Live Captions

Transcription runs on an offline recogniser on this device — audio is never sent to an online speech service. It hears this device's microphone (what you say), not the other people on the call. Captions are shown to you only unless you turn publishing on, they are never written to disk, and they never appear in notifications or logs.

⚠ Receive quality only affects mesh P2P calls (the default). If you switch audio to server relay, you get whatever the sender sends.

Sample rate for screen share audio sent via server relay. Only affects the sender's capture quality; screen audio volume is controlled separately per-member via right-click on the screen tile.

⚠ Receive screen audio quality only affects mesh P2P calls (the default). If you switch screen audio to server relay, you get whatever the sender sends.

Video Transmission

Everything (audio, camera and screen) uses P2P mesh by default — it is sent directly to each peer and never switches automatically, no matter how many people are in the channel.

To use the server relay instead, open the camera options menu (⋯) in a call and toggle Audio / Camera / Screen to Server relay. Relay is per-kind and always opt-in.

Video Quality

Send = the resolution you capture at. Receive = the resolution others send you (mesh P2P only — in server-relay mode, you receive at whatever the sender chose). Lower resolutions use less bandwidth. Defaults are deliberately low.

⚠ Receive resolutions only affect mesh P2P mode (the default). If you switch a feed to server relay, you receive at whatever the sender sends.

The frame rate every feed is sent at: the camera, your screen share (including the Android screen capture), the mesh encoders and the server relay all read this one value. Higher = smoother but uses more bandwidth. Changing it applies to every sender immediately; a live camera restarts at the new rate, while a live screen share keeps its rate until it is next started.

JPEG compression for relayed video frames. Lower = smaller blobs = less bandwidth but more blocky artifacts. Only affects server-relayed video, not mesh P2P.

When on, every camera and screen share shows a Load button until you click it — independently per person and per feed. You decide which feeds to load; senders keep sending regardless. Right-click (volume, mirror/rotate, owner controls) works everywhere, even on unloaded feeds.

If a camera or screen share stops encoding (a dropped negotiation can leave it black), the app renegotiates automatically after this many seconds of no frames and shows a brief Reconnecting video… notice. Off disables auto-recovery.

Advanced — Call Diagnostics

Live getStats for every peer in the current call/voice channel: frames encoded/decoded, packet loss, and whether the E2EE media transform is attached to each sender/receiver. A black feed usually shows 0 frames decoded on the receiving side or a missing E2EE badge.

Rejoin & heal first adds any missing E2EE transforms in place (no interruption); if a peer is still failed/disconnected or a video feed is still black, it leaves and rejoins the room so every connection is rebuilt from scratch.

Not in a call — join a voice channel or DM call to see per-peer stats.
